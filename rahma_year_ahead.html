Protect the time budget, don't just track it. At 10–15 hrs/week, the real risk isn't running out of roadmap — it's the support inbox, or one thread of feedback, quietly eating the whole week. Sequencing contractor help by what actually protects your time first:

  • Support, first. Most unbounded, least specialized knowledge transfer. Once Phase 2 gives you real ticket volume and revenue, this is the first hire worth making.
  • Content, next. Once the website track starts (Month 4+), a contractor writing posts under your direction is a natural second hire.

Set an actual threshold now — e.g. "once MRR covers X hours/week of support help, I hire it" — so the decision is made in advance by a number, not in the moment by how tired you are. Quarterly, ask yourself directly: is this pace still sustainable? Hold, push, or pull back? A "3 on the way to 1" year only works if you're still standing at the end of it.

▸Threads, triage & boundaries

The rules that keep this plan from being renegotiated every week.

  • Run the full Pre-Release QA Checklist every release
  • Re-check Apple's in-review cap before bundling store changes
  • Triage: decided → build; trust bug → jumps the queue; new scope → its own track; small polish → batched
  • No major rebuild, no hiring plan, no built community, no aggressive paid spend this year

Every release: run the full Pre-Release QA Checklist — it exists because specific real bugs (Android product-ID mismatch, version-code regression, gating drift) have already happened once. Every store-copy or pricing change: re-check against Apple's cap on simultaneous in-review submissions before bundling changes together.

How to triage new ideas going forward — a rough filter so this roadmap doesn't get renegotiated every time a new idea shows up:

  • Already decided and scoped (like the Contraction Timer) → goes into the next available build window, doesn't need re-litigating
  • Trust/data-integrity gaps (like the child-deletion cascade question) → treated like bugs, not features — fixed before new build, regardless of size
  • Genuinely new scope (like the website) → gets its own track with its own start condition, not squeezed into an existing phase
  • Small polish (folder rename/delete, character limits, minor copy) → batched into whatever release is already happening, never its own milestone

Deliberately not in Year 1: no major platform rebuild, no team hiring plan, no aggressive paid-growth spend before you have your own conversion data, no built-in community/forum feature (support is external links + partnerships instead), no chasing "AI narrative" or agentic features beyond the already-planned insight generation, and no full editorial content operation on the website. The current scope — app plus a lean content/partnership track — is already substantial for one part-time founder to ship well.
